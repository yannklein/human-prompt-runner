1. Technical & Domain Credibility (Non-Negotiable)

What I’d test

  • Do they understand where the real bottlenecks are (error correction, yield, cryogenics, control electronics, compilation, benchmarking)?
  • Can they distinguish physics risk from engineering risk and market risk?
  • Do they understand why timelines are long without defaulting to hand-wavy “10 years to revenue” narratives?

Signals

  • Prior investments in quantum or adjacent deep tech (semiconductors, photonics, advanced materials, HPC).
  • Partners/advisors with PhDs or hands-on experience—not just “former Google X”.
  • The quality of questions in diligence: precise, uncomfortable, and technical.

Red flags

  • Over-fixation on qubit counts without discussion of fidelity, connectivity, or error rates.
  • Treating quantum like SaaS with delayed ARR.


2. Time Horizon & Fund Construction Fit

Quantum companies die more often from VC impatience than from bad physics.

What I’d assess

  • Fund size vs. check size vs. ownership targets.
  • Whether they can follow on through Series C/D without scrambling.
  • LP expectations: are they actually comfortable with 10–15 year liquidity?

Questions I’d ask

  • “What’s the longest time to liquidity you’ve supported?”
  • “How do you think about markups when milestones are scientific, not revenue-based?”
  • “Have you shut down a company too early before—and why?”

Green flags

  • Evergreen funds, corporate VCs with strategic patience, or deep-tech-specialist funds.
  • Clear internal consensus on long timelines.


3. Platform Value Beyond Capital

In quantum, money is table stakes; access is leverage.

What matters

  • Talent pipelines: connections to top labs, postdocs, and industry engineers.
  • Strategic partners: hyperscalers, national labs, semiconductor fabs, aerospace, pharma.
  • Non-dilutive capital: grants, government programs, consortia (DARPA, EU, NSF, Innovate UK).

Concrete tests

  • “Who would you introduce us to in the first 30 days?”
  • “Which of your portfolio companies share suppliers, facilities, or customers with us?”
  • “Have you helped a deep-tech company secure non-VC funding before?”


4. Signaling & Narrative Discipline

Quantum lives under a microscope. Your investors define how you’re perceived.

I’d evaluate

  • Their reputation with later-stage investors and strategics.
  • Their relationship with press and analysts—and whether they overhype.
  • Their ability to help craft a credible, conservative narrative.

Strong signals

  • Known for under-promising and over-delivering.
  • Trusted by government and enterprise buyers.
  • Can help say “no” to bad demos, premature benchmarks, or hypey claims.

Red flags

  • Pushing for splashy announcements before technical readiness.
  • Encouraging misleading comparisons to classical compute.


5. Risk Tolerance & Governance Style

Quantum startups face long periods of ambiguity. Board dynamics matter enormously.

I’d probe

  • How they behave when milestones slip for legitimate technical reasons.
  • Their tolerance for pivots (e.g., hardware → control stack → software).
  • Whether they optimize for company survival or mark-to-market optics.

Questions I’d ask founders they backed

  • “How did they react when something fundamental didn’t work?”
  • “Did they push for premature commercialization?”
  • “Were they constructive during down rounds or extensions?”
